About

Turning HR complexity into products people actually want to use

I build products at the intersection of People, Product, and Technology — helping organizations transform complex HR processes into intuitive, scalable experiences that make work better for everyone.

With 11+ years of experience in People Technology, I've led enterprise HR products from stakeholder discovery and strategy through implementation, adoption, and production at Fortune 500 scale. My work spans Human Capital Management, Talent Acquisition, applicant tracking, workforce planning, and the employee lifecycle — from candidate experience to career development and retention.

I'm particularly passionate about the next generation of HR technology: GenAI, LLM-powered workflows, predictive talent intelligence, and AI copilots that help recruiters, hiring managers, and employees get answers and take action — not just navigate another dashboard.

I believe great products should deliver measurable business impact, whether that's cost savings, improved efficiency, or stronger adoption. Just as importantly, I believe in building high-performing teams and scalable solutions that continue to create value long after launch.

My focus: Simplify complexity. Build what matters. Deliver impact.

Long before I started building HR technology, I was learning the fundamentals of understanding people.

At 15, I took my first job at Pizza Hut. It was a simple environment, but it taught me something that has stayed with me throughout my career: people don't always tell you exactly what they need. Sometimes you have to listen carefully, read the situation, and figure out how to deliver the right experience — especially when the dinner rush is in full swing.

In college, I worked in retail banking at Bank of America and later TD Bank, where I began building genuine relationships with customers and helping them find financial products aligned with their goals. It was my first real exposure to translating someone's needs into a solution that made sense for them.

A six-month co-op as a Compliance Analyst at Valley Forge Asset Management introduced me to corporate structures, legal teams, investment advisors, and the inner workings of financial products. I learned how important it is to understand the rules, the risks, and the details behind the systems people rely on.

Later, during a six-month stint on the technology team at Exelon PECO, I helped convert paper-based utility field maps into digital maps and trained frontline linemen to use Panasonic Toughbooks and the new technology in the field. It was an early lesson in digital transformation — because building a new system is only part of the job. The real impact comes when people can understand it, trust it, and use it to do their work better.

Different industries. Different problems. Different people.
But the thread connecting all of those experiences has remained the same: understand what people actually need, then build or explain the thing that helps them get there.
